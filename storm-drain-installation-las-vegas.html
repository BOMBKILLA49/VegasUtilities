What's included in storm drain & drainage work

Because of the Las Vegas Valley's flash-flood risk, drainage is one of the most closely reviewed parts of any site plan. Contractors we match you with typically handle:

  • Storm drain pipe and catch basin installation
  • Detention and retention basin construction, including outlet structures
  • Site drainage grading, swales, and overland flow paths
  • Connections to public storm drain systems
  • Erosion control and NPDES stormwater pollution prevention compliance during construction
  • Coordination with the local jurisdiction and flood control district on design standards

Why drainage design is different in the Las Vegas Valley

Southern Nevada's desert climate means rain is infrequent but can be intense, and the Valley's terrain channels stormwater quickly through washes and urban drainage systems, producing fast-moving flash floods during summer monsoon storms (roughly July through September). Because of this risk, storm drain and site drainage designs are reviewed against standards developed with input from the Clark County Regional Flood Control District, in addition to each jurisdiction's own requirements. Getting this design right the first time avoids plan review delays — see our permitting overview.

Detention vs. retention basins

A detention basin temporarily holds stormwater during a storm and releases it slowly afterward, typically staying dry between events — the more common requirement on commercial sites. A retention basin holds water for a longer period, sometimes permanently, and may double as a landscape or infiltration feature. Which one your site needs (and how it's sized) is determined by your civil engineer based on local drainage study requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Why does storm drain design matter so much in the Las Vegas Valley?

The Valley receives infrequent but sometimes intense rainfall, particularly during the summer monsoon season, which can produce fast-moving flash flooding. Because of this, storm drain and drainage design is closely reviewed against standards developed with the Clark County Regional Flood Control District.

What's the difference between detention and retention basins?

A detention basin temporarily holds stormwater and releases it slowly after a storm, typically staying dry between events. A retention basin is designed to hold water for a longer period or permanently, and may be used for infiltration or aesthetic ponds.

Do I need a stormwater permit during construction?

Projects that disturb a certain amount of land typically need to comply with NPDES stormwater pollution prevention requirements administered through the Nevada Division of Environmental Protection, in addition to local grading and drainage permits.

When is storm drain typically installed relative to sewer and water?

Storm drain is commonly installed after sanitary sewer, since sewer's gravity-flow depth often drives site layout, but sequencing can vary by project and civil engineering design.
