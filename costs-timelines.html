Typical costs & timelines for site work in Las Vegas

Every site is different, so treat the numbers below as general planning references — not a quote. They're meant to help you budget realistically before you talk to contractors.

Why we can't give you an exact number here

Earthwork and utility pricing depends on soil conditions, site access, depth of cut/fill, distance to haul-off or import sites, current fuel and material costs, and which jurisdiction and utility district is involved. The ranges below are general, order-of-magnitude figures to help with early budgeting — your actual cost could fall outside them. A site visit and a real quote from a vetted contractor is the only way to get an accurate number for your project.

What drives cost on a Las Vegas Valley site

  • Soil conditions — caliche and rock layers common in the Valley can require ripping or blasting, which adds cost and time compared to easily excavated soil.
  • Cut/fill balance — a site that needs large amounts of soil hauled off (or imported) costs more than one where cut and fill roughly balance on site.
  • Depth and length of utility runs — deeper trenches and longer runs mean more excavation, shoring, bedding material, and labor hours.
  • Site access — tight urban lots or sites with limited equipment access typically cost more than open parcels.
  • Utility district requirements — some agencies require specific pipe materials, bedding, or testing that can affect cost.
  • Time of year — summer heat can affect crew productivity and scheduling in Southern Nevada; monsoon season (roughly July–September) can also cause weather delays on open excavations.

Typical project timelines

Timelines depend heavily on project size and permitting complexity, but here's a general sequence for a mid-size commercial site in the Las Vegas Valley:

PhaseGeneral duration
Civil engineering & permittingWeeks to several months, depending on jurisdiction and plan review cycles
Mobilization & clearingA few days to a couple weeks
Mass excavation & rough grading1–4+ weeks depending on site size and soil
Underground utility installation2–8+ weeks depending on scope and number of trades
Testing, inspection & agency acceptanceDays to a few weeks, depending on inspector availability
Fine grading & final site prep1–2 weeks

Permitting is usually the biggest source of schedule uncertainty — see our permitting and inspections overview for what affects that timeline most.

How to budget responsibly

  1. Get a geotechnical report early — it removes a lot of the guesswork around soil-related costs.
  2. Build in contingency (commonly 10–20% on earthwork/utility scopes) for unforeseen conditions like buried debris, unexpected rock, or utility conflicts.
  3. Get more than one quote from properly licensed, vetted contractors so you can compare scope, not just price.
  4. Ask what's excluded from a bid — haul-off, dewatering, and traffic control are common items that get left out of low bids.
