What's included in full site development

Many Las Vegas Valley developers prefer a single contractor to handle grading and all underground utilities together, since the sequencing between these scopes is tightly linked. A full site development package typically includes:

  • Site clearing, mass excavation, and rough grading
  • Sanitary sewer main and lateral installation
  • Domestic and fire service water line installation
  • Storm drain, detention/retention, and site drainage systems
  • Fine grading and final site preparation for pavement, foundations, and landscaping
  • Coordination with dry utility providers (power, gas, telecom) where applicable

Need detail on a specific piece of this? See our dedicated pages for earthwork & grading, sewer line installation, water line installation, and storm drain & drainage.

Why sequencing is the whole point of "full" site development

On a typical Las Vegas Valley project, rough grading establishes stable, accessible ground; sanitary sewer generally goes in first since it relies on gravity flow and is often the deepest line; storm drain and water follow; and fine grading finishes the site for paving and vertical construction. When one contractor manages this whole sequence, conflicts between trades — like utility trenches cutting through freshly graded subgrade — are far less common than when an owner is coordinating several separate contractors directly.

When a combined package makes sense

  • You're developing a raw or vacant parcel that needs both grading and all underground utilities
  • You want a single point of contact and one coordinated schedule instead of managing multiple trades
  • Your project has a tight timeline where sequencing delays would be costly
  • You want one contractor accountable for the site being fully construction-ready at hand-off

If you only need one specific scope — say, just a sewer connection or just grading — our individual service pages may be a better fit.

Typical timeline

A combined grading-and-utility package on a mid-size Las Vegas Valley commercial site commonly takes several weeks to a few months from mobilization through final agency acceptance, depending on site size, soil conditions, and permitting timelines. Frequently asked questions

What does "full site development" mean?

Full site development (also called site preparation) refers to combining earthwork/grading with underground utility installation — sewer, water, and storm drain — under one coordinated scope, so the site is fully ready for vertical construction hand-off.

Why do developers combine grading and utilities into one contract?

Because these phases are tightly sequenced — utilities generally go in after rough grading but before final grading and paving — a single contractor managing both reduces coordination gaps, scheduling conflicts, and finger-pointing between trades.

Does site development include permitting?

Contractors typically manage construction-phase permitting and inspections for the scopes they perform, though civil engineering plan preparation is usually handled by a separate civil engineer earlier in the process.

How long does a full site development project take?

It varies by site size and scope, but a combined grading and utility package on a mid-size commercial site in the Las Vegas Valley commonly takes several weeks to a few months from mobilization to final acceptance. See our costs and timelines guide for a phase-by-phase breakdown.
